STEM Racing is the world's largest STEM competition. Teams of three to six design a miniature compressed-air car, manufacture it, and race it over twenty metres in about one second. The car is only half the marks. The rest comes from engineering documentation, sponsorship, branding and how you present under questioning.

What you actually make

A season is five deliverables, not one car.

Teams who only think about the car lose to teams who understood the whole brief. Every one of these is judged, and every one of them needs someone on your team who owns it.

01

The car

Designed in CAD, checked against the technical regulations, machined and finished to tolerance.

02

Engineering portfolio

Your design reasoning, testing, simulation and manufacturing evidence, written up as a formal document.

03

Enterprise portfolio

Budget, sponsorship, brand identity and marketing. Run the team like a business, because it is one.

04

Pit display

A physical stand you design and build, staffed by your team while judges walk the paddock.

05

Verbal presentation

Ten minutes in front of a judging panel, followed by questions you cannot rehearse for.

How to apply

Three steps. Roughly an evening's work.

One submission per team. Nominate a Team Manager to fill in the form on everyone's behalf, but write the answers together.

Step one

Form a team of 3 to 6

Pick people who will still be turning up in March, not just the people you sit with. You will need someone comfortable in CAD, someone who can design, and someone who will chase sponsors.

Step two

Read the rubric, then answer against it

Every question on the form maps to a criterion below. Score yourselves honestly first and fix the weak answers before you submit.

Step three

Submit before 21 August

Shortlisted teams are invited to a short interview. It is a conversation about your proposal, not a test. Open the proposal form →

The judging sheet

Mark your own proposal before we do.

This is the exact sheet the panel uses. Drag along each row, or tap a band, to pick the one that honestly describes your answer. If you land under 70, you know which section to rewrite.

Two panel members mark independently and the scores are averaged. The panel is not looking for teams who already know how to design a race car. It is looking for teams who will still be here in March. Honesty scores higher than polish throughout.
